# How I Led This

I led the move from Lex to Decagon from pilot to production, alongside the customer experience team, in about three and a half to four months of pilot to production work.

## The hats I wore

- **Discovery lead.** Mapped where customers got stuck under Lex and which servicing requests drove the most volume.
- **Buyer side evaluator.** Compared vendors and made the case for Decagon over Sierra, offered through Accenture, because Varo would keep control of its data, content, and workflows.
- **Program lead.** Set the rollout plan, a small share of customers first, then step by step to 100% of chat traffic by July 2026.
- **Data and analytics lead.** Owned the data export, the pipelines, the monitors, and the dashboards that told us whether it was working.
- **Quality owner.** Set up the small test set and the daily chat review with the quality team.
- **Partner to engineering, security, and legal.** Worked through the account connections, privacy masking, and dispute reviews with each team.

## Earning trust early and finding who matters

I partnered with the customer experience product manager from day one and brought in the executive sponsor, engineering, security, legal, analytics, and quality early. Each group had a different definition of success: fewer handoffs for customer experience, safe account connections for engineering, clean reviews for legal, and trustworthy numbers for leadership. I kept those in view side by side.

## Hearing the need behind the ask

The ask was a better chatbot. The need was servicing that scales through the January to March busy season without cost growing at the same rate, on a system Varo owns. That is why ownership of data and workflows weighed as heavily as product quality in the vendor choice.

## Talking early and often

When a data format problem blocked the pipeline in late January, I raised it the same week. When secure account connections ran about 10 days late, engineering pulled the security work forward. When early resolution trailed Lex, I said so plainly and explained why, instead of letting leadership find out later.

## Not over-promising

I kept the test set to about 30 real cases instead of 300, because a bigger set of automated checks creates noise and slows the team down. I launched to a small share of customers first and grew it step by step. I treated the dispute cost reduction as a target, not a result, until it is measured.

## Setting the team up to do their best work

Customer experience owned the workflows and the customer voice. Engineering owned the account connections. Analytics owned the dashboards. Quality owned the daily review. My job was to make sure each group could move fast on its part and that the pieces landed together.

## Outcomes

- 100% of in-app chat traffic on Decagon by July 2026.
- Card questions resolved without a person rose from 44% in April to 57% in July 2026.
- Net promoter score rose from about 40 to 50 to over 80, based on my own reporting.
- A clear next step for servicing: card actions in chat, then dispute intake with a target of about 40% lower cost per dispute.

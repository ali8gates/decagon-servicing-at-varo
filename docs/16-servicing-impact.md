# What This Changed for Servicing

Most of what customers contact Varo about is servicing: their card, their account, their money, and problems with a transaction. This page maps the Decagon work to that side of the business.

## Where servicing volume comes from

"I need help with my card" is the top contact reason in the in-app chat (Weekly team sync, data product, Jul 8, 2026). Under Lex, every one of those conversations had to fit a fixed script, and anything outside it went to a person. Some requests, like closing an account, were not supported at all (Decagon company training, Apr 3, 2026).

## How each servicing area moved

| Servicing area | Under Lex | With Decagon | Where it stands |
|---|---|---|---|
| Card questions | Scripted answers, frequent handoffs | Plain language questions answered from help content and account data | Resolved without a person rose from 44% in April to 57% in July 2026 |
| Card actions (activate, freeze) | Handled by a person | Next step: done inside the chat | First priority on the roadmap |
| Account servicing (balance, closing an account) | Limited or not supported | Added once secure account connections were live | Live in chat after the connections landed about 10 days late |
| Dispute intake | Manual, slow, and costly | Next step: collect dispute details automatically | Target of about 40% lower cost per dispute in the third quarter of 2026 |
| Handoff to a person | Customer often repeated themselves | Chat summary and full history passed to the human agent through Amazon Connect | Live |
| Phone and signed out customers | Not covered | Not covered yet | On the roadmap |

## What servicing agents gained

- Fewer routine card and account questions reaching the queue, so agents spend more time on the cases that need judgment.
- A summary and full history on every handoff, so agents start with context instead of asking the customer to start over.
- A daily review of a sample of chats, roughly 10 to 50 a day, so problems get caught by people who know Varo's servicing policies (Decagon company training, Apr 3, 2026).

## Why the order mattered

I sequenced servicing by risk. Help content first, because a wrong answer there is easy to correct. Account actions next, only after security and privacy reviews signed off. Disputes last, because a dispute touches money and regulation and deserves the most care. That order is also why early resolution trailed Lex, 43% versus 55%, before account actions were added. I shared that gap openly at the time instead of waiting for the numbers to catch up.

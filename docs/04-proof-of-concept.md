# Proof of concept and vendor choice

## What we tested

In the first demo, Decagon walked through the kinds of questions Varo customers ask most: account balances, when deposited money becomes available, direct deposit status, and whether a customer qualifies for a product. The team compared this against how Lex handled the same questions (varo <> Decagon, Oct 20, 2025).

## Options we weighed

| Option | What it offered | Concern |
|---|---|---|
| Keep Amazon Lex | Already live, known cost | Scripted, hard to extend, weaker customer experience |
| Sierra, through Accenture | Strong product | A partner would control a key customer touch point and our brand voice (Customer service partner next steps, Nov 12, 2025) |
| Decagon | Building blocks Varo can shape and own; shared investor | New vendor, new data formats |

## Why Decagon won

From the November 12, 2025 decision meeting (Customer service partner next steps, Nov 12, 2025):

* **Control.** Varo writes and owns its own workflows, content, and rules.
* **Flexibility.** The platform is built from pieces that can be changed one at a time.
* **Price.** The cost fit a budget that was already approved.
* **Relationship.** Warburg Pincus invests in both companies, which helped open doors with the founding team.
* **Timing.** The team needed to be ready for the January to March busy season.

## How fast it moved

I ran the whole effort, from pilot to production, in about three and a half to four months.
